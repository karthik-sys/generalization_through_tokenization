Mixture-of-Tokenizers — build console

Does domain-routed, multi-tokenizer LLM architecture beat unified BPE at matched small scale (68–190M params)? 5 base architectures, 11 arms total counting variants and scale tests.

Headline: MoT-family arms don't just win on paper — routed generates real domain switches behaviorally (seeded in science, it emits a switch token and writes valid Python, then LaTeX, then prose), and it posts the best out-of-domain generalization of any arm (LAMBADA perplexity 48 vs unified-BPE baseline's 7,786 and cl100k SOTA's 72,149). Full detail below.

The scoreboard — evaluated arms

Two metrics, two different questions. BPB (bits-per-byte, lower=better) = in-domain compression on held-out text from the training distribution. LAMBADA (target-token perplexity, lower=better) = predicting genuinely novel English the model was never shaped around. They disagree, and the disagreement is the story.

Generation — the thesis, shown behaviorally

Switch-prediction accuracy sits at chance (~26%), which reads as "can't predict switches." But that measures foresight. Free-running generation shows the real capability: routed acts on the switch mechanism and produces domain-appropriate content after each switch. Diagnostic, not demo — at 89M params the prose is GPT-2-small-rough; the switching behavior is the signal. switch→X marks a real table switch the model chose to emit.

Three self-chosen switches: physics → a math-help post with LaTeX integrals → back to physics → and into real Python (import torch.nn as nn). Each switch flips to that domain's own tokenizer + embedding table + head. This is the mechanism our metrics couldn't see.

Surprisingly coherent arxiv-flavored physics/math at 89M params — real LaTeX, real concepts (Hecke algebras, quantum Hall plateaus). Stays in science the whole time, as it must: MoT commits to one domain per forward pass.

What we've learned

Disjoint tables beat unified vocab on generalization, decisively. baseline/sota collapse on LAMBADA (7,786 / 72,149 ppl) while MoT-family sit at 55–283. A shared vocab splits capacity across all 4 domains; MoT gives each domain's table its full capacity. Invisible on in-domain BPB, brutal off-distribution. Confound-checked — restoring the training-format domain tag moved every arm <5%.
Routing's payoff is behavioral, not predictive. Switch accuracy is at chance, yet routed generates genuine domain switches with correct content. The capability isn't foreseeing switches — it's reconfiguring instantly when one lands. That reframes the metric, and it's why the denser-switch routed3 experiment is worth running.
SOTA's 100k vocab is a liability at this scale, not an asset. 102M of its 122M params sit in embedding/head for a vocabulary mostly irrelevant to this corpus — more classes to learn well, less data per class. Tokenizer sophistication lost to tokenizer fit under constrained compute.
Open question — does it survive scale? Every result is at 68–122M params, exactly where tokenizer-specialization effects show strongest. The next real bet is a matched MoT-vs-baseline pair at 350M–1B to see if the gap holds or shrinks. Being thoughtful before spending there.
