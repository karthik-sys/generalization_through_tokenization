## Part 1 — Retrospective: what we actually got done

Eight phases, each ending in a decision that shaped the next. EM = LAMBADA exact-match.

**Phase 0 — Thesis (mot / baseline / sota / pooled / hybrid, 150k steps each).**
Question: does routing per-domain tokenizers beat one unified vocab?
Result: unified BPE compresses better (baseline 2.00 BPB) but is *incapable* of LAMBADA (0.0%); the routed arm became champion (47.8 ppl vs baseline's 7,786). cl100k ("sota" arm) is a pure liability at this scale.
**Decision that stuck: the compression/generalization trade-off is the project's central object.**

**Phase 1 — Loss-function detour (routed2/3/4, pooled2). All failures, all informative.**
GradNorm improved switch accuracy but wrecked LM quality in three independent variants; a gradient-decoupled switch head learned nothing (0.000 switch acc). **Lesson: don't fix the switch problem in the loss function.**

**Phase 2 — Scale test (routed-large vs baseline-large, 190M/160M, matched 150k).**
The architectural advantage *survives and sharpens* at scale: 34x LAMBADA gap in our favor, but baseline-large still wins single BPB (1.354, still the project's best). **Scale doesn't resolve the trade-off; it magnifies both sides.**

**Phase 3 — Data levers (routed7/8/9/10).**
OWT-sourcing nlp: small real win. 4x data volume (routed8, 600k steps, 89M): closed most of the scale gap alone — EM 6%, our first nonzero-EM era. Volume plateaued by 575k. **Lesson: data > steps > scale, in that order, at this size.**

**Phase 4 — Mechanism bets (routed11–18) + the two measurement catastrophes.**
Found and fixed the **copy-gate eval bug** (every copy-gate arm had been scored as its parent — the mechanism worked all along: routed11 jumped to 9.9% EM once actually measured) and the **n=500 LAMBADA bias** (unshuffled easy head; all prior numbers ~0.5pp optimistic). Diet (70% nlp) discovered: +3pp EM (routed17). **Two of the project's four biggest wins were measurement fixes, not models.**

**Phase 5 — Curriculum (routed19, 300k, H100). Rejected, conclusively.**
Phase-1 single-domain training produced the architecture's best-ever BPB (1.355 ≈ baseline-large) — then phase-2 switching erased it monotonically across four checkpoints. **Lesson: the architecture CAN compress like a unified model; simultaneous mixing, not staging, is how skills must be co-trained. The 1.355 existence proof drives this week's routed36.**

**Phase 6 — Ablation night (routed20–24).**
Clean factorial: gate+diet stack is super-additive (routed21: 14.75% vs ~9-10% for either alone); alignment loss is neutral-to-catastrophic (rejected); full plasticity a modest +0.5pp. **routed21 became the canonical recipe: simplest thing that wins.**

**Phase 7 — Longer + bigger (routed25–28).**
routed25 (stack, 300k): **16.57% EM, best confirmed full-set number in the project.** routed26: diet >70% adds nothing. routed27 (books nlp): best 89M cross BPB, gentlest math/science erosion. routed28 (190M): matched 89M quality in <half the steps, best cross BPB (1.04) — but exposed **diet erosion**: single BPB worsened monotonically 1.58→1.80, math ppl 298→919. **Lesson: the flagship recipe overfits its diet; final checkpoints are not the best checkpoints.**

**Phase 8 — Reallocation batch (routed29–33, yesterday).**
B (direct tying) diverged — the batch's only true architecture failure. A infra-killed (untested). Three live wins, all unfinished: **C (modern backbone): 17.6% EM at 31% budget** — steepest trajectory ever; D (RoPE+RMSNorm): 16.6% at 74%; **routed33 (generalist 5th domain): 18.0% at 70%, from scratch, no gate-parent, plain backbone.** Also measured (not yet fixed): switch-loss vs LM-loss gradient conflict, cos −0.117, negative in 96.7% of batches.

**Project arc in one line:** EM 0.4% → 6% → 10% → 14.8% → 16.6% → ~18% at ≤222M params, driven by (in order of impact): measurement fixes, data volume, diet, copy-gate, stacking, scale, modern backbone, generalist domain.

## Part 2 — The $200 week (revised twice: fast infra landed, then corrected, 2026-08-20)

### Budget reality — corrected after a real-training-loop check caught an overclaim
The 4.4x below was real but measured wrong, and the mistake is worth stating plainly rather than quietly fixing: `calibrate --arm routed25 --steps 150` showed 0.526→0.120 sec/step. But `calibrate()` calls `opt.step()` on *every* micro-batch (no accumulation) — it does not exercise `train()`'s real loop, which only syncs the optimizer once every `GRAD_ACCUM_STEPS` (16, currently) micro-batches (`is_sync_step = step % grad_accum == 0`). Since bf16/TF32/fused's benefit concentrates almost entirely in optimizer-step overhead (GradScaler's scale/unscale/inf-check, fp32 master weights, non-fused AdamW), `calibrate()` was measuring a workload 16x more optimizer-heavy than what real training at the current batch/accum setting actually runs.

**Direct check, real `train()` loop, 500 steps, same seed (0), old code vs new code, routed25:** loss curves overlaid almost exactly (parity confirmed — 9.5153→7.2778 vs 9.5153→7.2776, no divergence, no level shift, the bf16 switch trains the same model). **Wall time: 60s vs 60s. No measurable speedup at the current batch=4/grad_accum=16 setting.**

This means fix #1 (micro-batch 4→32, grad-accum 16→2) isn't an independent additional lever on top of bf16/TF32/fused — **it's the precondition for the other three to matter at all.** At grad_accum=16, the optimizer-step overhead they target is already amortized over 16 micro-batches into a small fraction of total time. Cutting grad_accum to 2 makes optimizer-step frequency 8x more prominent relative to forward/backward volume, which is exactly where bf16/TF32/fused's savings would start showing up. Real, but **unvalidated as a combined number** — the batch/accum change itself only got a partially-converged `calibrate()` reading (0.328 sec/step, still dropping at 150 steps), and now that `calibrate()`'s methodology is known to overstate optimizer-bound gains, that number can't be trusted either.

**Correction: reverting the committed budget to the original, un-discounted estimate** until a real `train()`-loop benchmark (not `calibrate()`) is run with batch=32/accum=2 *combined* with bf16/TF32/fused, for long enough to reach steady state. That's cheap (another ~$0.02, a few minutes on a spare pod) and is the first thing to do when routed34's actual recipe is being wired up — not before.

A40 = $0.44/hr → $200 ≈ **454 A40-hours**. 89M/300k steps ≈ 44hrs ≈ **$19/arm** (unchanged from the original estimate); large-scale ≈ **$35–40/arm**. Modal T4 evals are pennies by comparison.

## Part 3 — "Working optimally": the standing QA regime

What we now do *always*, born of this project's own failure modes:

1. **Measurement first.** Full n=5153 for any ranking decision; n=500 only for mid-run pulse checks, always labeled. Bootstrap CI printed next to every EM. (Cost of learning this: every pre-correction number was wrong by ~0.5pp; the copy-gate sat mismeasured for two full rounds.)
2. **Compare by tokens-seen, never step-%.** Batch sizes differ across arms; step-matched comparisons flattered routed19 for weeks.
3. **Checkpoint selection is part of the recipe.** Eval every 50k; the deliverable is the *best-balanced* checkpoint, not the last one (routed28@140k > routed28@300k on the metric mix we actually care about).
4. **Per-domain BPB tracked at every eval** — math/science erosion is the leading indicator of diet damage, visible tens of thousands of steps before aggregate numbers move.
5. **One-variable arms with named controls.** Every recipe pairs against the arm it modifies (routed20/21, routed22/23 pattern). No multi-change arms without a factorial partner.
6. **Persist results the moment they exist.** Every eval lands in `metrics.json` + a pushed summary the same session (routed24–28 lived only in chat for a day — never again).
7. **Infra watchdogs:** auto-resume launcher on every pod (a cron re-execs training if the process dies — migrations silently killed 4 runs yesterday); balance alarm well above zero (a $0 balance stopped all pods at once); on-pod eval fallback for the Modal ModernBackbone cancellation bug.
8. **Kill criteria written before launch** (see Part 2) — GPU-hours go to live questions, not sunk costs.
9. **Gradient-conflict diagnostic run once per new arm** — cheap, and it caught a real, measurable pathology (−0.117 cos) that this week's routed37 finally tests.
10. **Idle = off.** No pod runs without a training process on it; verified at every check-in, not assumed.
